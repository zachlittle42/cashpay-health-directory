# South Korea medical tourism: clinic-level dossier (research notes)

Prepared for VitalityScout. Research date: 2026-10-30 (all URLs accessed that day unless noted). Audience: American cash-pay reader.

## Method and caveats

- Primary method: read each provider's own English (or Korean) site pages with WebFetch; cross-checked credentials against the Medical Korea portal (medicalkorea.or.kr, run by KHIDI) on 2026-10-30; sanctions/incidents from Korean press in English.
- "Not published" = I looked at the pages listed and did not find it. It does not mean the clinic has no such policy.
- Prices: only what a clinic's own site shows is labeled "clinic-published." Marketplace figures (Bookimed, Creatrip, Trazy, Klook, PlacidWay) are labeled "marketplace" and should not be presented as clinic prices.
- The web-search budget for this session ran out partway through; the remaining work was done by direct page fetches. Items I could not reach are flagged.
- Pages I could not load (as of 2026-10-30): JK "How to use JK" guide (404); DA English site daprseng.com (renders empty, JavaScript-only); Lienjang (DNS failure on lienjang.com / www.lienjang.com; connection refused on lienjang.co.kr); Oracle Cheongdam branch site (403); Seoul St. Mary's English pages (404/500); Braun Plastic Surgery English site (404); engbanobagi.com (expired TLS certificate; eng.banobagi.com worked); Wonjin doctors page (rendered nav only).

### Credential lists checked (all 2026-10-30)

Shows "Total 26" institutions on one page. Program page (https://www.medicalkorea.or.kr/en/kahf) says: pilot 2016; 4th cycle 2023-2026; criteria = foreign-patient system (44) + patient safety (92); benefits include automatic Ministry of Justice "excellent medical tourism institution" designation. Press (idaegu.com, 2026-01-08) describes the current scheme as 144 items in five domains, on-site survey by the Korea Institute for Healthcare Accreditation (KOIHA), certification review by KHIDI, term about four years. Of the providers below, only **JK Plastic Surgery Center**, **AB Plastic Surgery**, and **KMI Korea Central Clinic** appear on the dedicated 26-entry KAHF list. Important: the portal's site-search results tab is labeled "Most Visited · KAHF Accredited Medical Institutions" as a combined heading; appearing there does NOT by itself mean KAHF accreditation. I relied on the dedicated 26-entry list for KAHF status.
3. **Medical Korea "Most Visited Hospitals"** list (151 institutions; page does not define the ranking): https://www.medicalkorea.or.kr/en/mostvisitedmedicalinstitutions. Page 1 included Asan Medical Center, B&VIIT Eye Center, Banobagi Plastic Surgery.
4. **JCI**: checked only via the clinic/hospital's own pages; none of the providers below state JCI on the pages I read except that the KAHF list shows "KAHF; JCI" for Kim Byoung Joon LEDAS Varicose Vein Clinic (not in scope). Treat JCI as "not stated on site" everywhere below unless noted.

### Sanctions and incidents (press)

- **FTC corrective orders, July 12, 2026**: Korea Fair Trade Commission issued corrective orders to View Plastic Surgery (Gangnam), DA Plastic Surgery (Gangnam) and AB Plastic Surgery Clinic (Seocho) for having paid "models" (paid via surgery discounts) post undisclosed reviews on beauty apps, cafes and the clinics' own sites; View and DA took deposits from models refunded only after reviews were posted. Violation periods: View April 2021 to May 2026; AB 2021 to May 2026; DA 2018 to November 2025. View must also publicly disclose the order. Suspected Medical Service Act violations were referred to MOHW. Sources: Kyunghyang Shinmun English (khan.co.kr, 2026-07-12) and Korea Times (2026-07-13). No fines reported.
- **FTC prepaid-treatment refund terms, July 19, 2026**: FTC had 15 unnamed dermatology/plastic surgery clinics (top 15 by consumer-agency complaint volume 2023-2024) voluntarily fix six unfair clause types (no-refund-after-dissatisfaction clauses, 20-30% cancellation fees, liability disclaimers, etc.). New standard: on mid-course cancellation, refund balance minus completed procedures and a 10% fee. Source: SBS News English (news.sbs.co.kr). Clinics not named.
- **Gangnam liposuction death (Jan 2024) and June 2026 indictment**: A Chinese woman in her 20s died Jan 10, 2024 after liposuction at an unnamed Gangnam clinic; the Chinese embassy in Seoul issued a WeChat warning (daum.net/Reuters, 2024-01-23). The Herald Business (2026-06-15) reported prosecutors indicted the clinic director ("A", 50s) without detention on June 11, 2026 for professional negligence resulting in death; prosecutors said A had already been indicted (May 2023) over a 2018 death of another Chinese liposuction patient from failure to monitor under anesthesia. **The clinic is not named in either report**, so no provider below can be linked to it.
- **Ghost surgery**: Annals of Surgical Treatment and Research (2018) describes the practice as "rampant" in Korean cosmetic surgery and notes foreign patients affected (no figures). Grand Plastic Surgery's safety page advertises a "surgical name-verification system" (수술실명제), implying this remains a live consumer concern.

## PART 2: CONTEXT QUESTIONS

### (a) What foreign patients go to Korea for; what Americans go for

**2025 national figures (MOHW data released April 24, 2026, reported by Korea JoongAng Daily 2026-04-24; Korea Biomedical Review 2026-10-08; Korea Herald 2026-05-19):**
- Total foreign patients 2025: 2.01 million (2,011,822 per secondary tally), from 201 countries; up about 72% from 1.17 million in 2024; first time above 2 million; low of ~110,000 in 2020.
- Department shares (counted per department, so one patient can appear in several): dermatology 62.9% (about 1.31 million, up 86.2% from 705,044); plastic surgery 11.2% (233,100, up 64.3%); internal medicine 9.2%. Dermatology + plastic surgery = 74.1% (68% in 2024, 52% in 2023), and 82.9% of the year's growth. Health screening and dental shares: not stated in the articles read (the brief's "3.1%" for screening could not be confirmed from a source I read; label it unverified).
- Nationality: China 30.8%, Japan 29.8%, Taiwan 9.2%, **United States 8.6% (173,363, up 70.4%, highest since 2009 records began)**, Thailand 2.9%.
- Region: Seoul 87.2%, Busan 2.8%, Jeju 2.3%.
- VAT refund scheme for foreign cosmetic patients: KRW 196.4 billion refunded in 2025 (1.72 million refunds); scheme began April 2016 and **expired at the end of 2025** (Korea Biomed). Clinics quoting a "10% tax refund" are citing a benefit that no longer applies as of 2026, unless renewed.

**Americans specifically**: Korea Biomed (2026-10-08) reports that dermatology + plastic surgery accounted for 53.6% of U.S. patients' department counts, far below Taiwan (89%), Japan (82.8%), China (82.5%). A secondary source citing the same MOHW release (Breeze in Busan, 2026-07-20) gives U.S. shares of dermatology 44.3%, integrated internal medicine 13.2%, plastic surgery 9.3%; MOHW does not publish reason for visit, residence or insurance status, so U.S. figures include expatriates and military-affiliated residents, not only cash-pay travelers. Korea Times (2026-02-25, 2024 data) counts about 100,000 U.S. patients in 2024 and lists top motivations overall as technology/outcomes, facilities, one-stop diagnosis and treatment, and airport access. Cash-price anecdotes for Americans (e.g., screening trips) exist only in dated or promotional sources.

### (b) Result expectations and revision context

**Rhinoplasty** (American Society of Plastic Surgeons articles, plasticsurgery.org): splint and sutures out in the first week; under-eye bruising mostly gone by one week; normal activity after 2-3 weeks; about half of swelling gone at 6 weeks; "up to 40% of the swelling" may remain at 1-3 months (volumetric data cited by Dr. Weinfeld); tip swelling lasts longest, especially after open rhinoplasty; nose may look uneven for up to a year; "full healing can actually take one to two years." ASPS cites a *Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery – Global Open* study putting the rhinoplasty revision rate "between 5-15%." Wonjin's FAQ (clinic source) says nasal tissues take about 6 months to recover and revision is more complex than primary surgery. Implication for a traveler: a 7-10 day trip ends well before results are judgeable, and any revision decision should wait roughly a year.

**Double eyelid (Asian blepharoplasty)**: View's precautions page (clinic source): significant swelling 1-2 weeks, major swelling down by ~1 month, residual swelling decreasing over ~6 months. Wonjin FAQ (clinic): asymmetry should not be judged before 6 months because swelling persists that long. Peer-reviewed: a 2024 Chinese retrospective series (Frontiers in Medicine; 333 patients) reported complication rates 12.3% (small-incision buried suture) vs 36.0% (full incision), recurrence/fold loss 7.8% vs 3.4% (not significant), satisfaction 95.5% vs 86.5% at 6 months. A 2010 Archives of Facial Plastic Surgery review of corrective surgery after unsatisfactory double-eyelid results concludes complications "should be avoided at the first blepharoplasty, because corrective surgery is difficult." No Korean population-level revision rate was found.

**Facial contouring (zygoma / mandible reduction)**: View (clinic): swelling increases 3-4 days, daily activity by days 5-7, ~80% of swelling gone by one month, CT/X-ray checks across 1-6 months. Wonjin (clinic): ~80% of swelling down in 7 days, 2 weeks off for talking-heavy jobs; revision ~6 months after primary. Peer-reviewed: Archives of Plastic Surgery (2008; Yang et al.) reviewed 53 Korean patients needing secondary surgery after reduction malarplasty elsewhere; causes were undercorrection of the arch with overcorrection of the body (28/53), arch undercorrection (8), asymmetry (4), malunion (2), nonunion (2). Known complication types: undercorrection/overcorrection, asymmetry, cheek drooping (soft-tissue sag that may appear only months later once swelling resolves), malunion/nonunion, numbness (most common after mandibuloplasty in a short-follow-up series). Pooled complication rates from the 2017 and 2023 meta-analyses could not be retrieved (paywall). Message for readers: final contour is a 6-month judgment, soft-tissue sagging is a late risk, and bone revision is a second major surgery.

### (c) Consultation-to-surgery timing for foreigners, and the flagged risk

- **What clinics themselves say**: Wonjin's safety page says its in-house pre-op center (ECG, blood, X-ray) "allow[s] same-day operations." B&VIIT offers eye exam in the morning and surgery in the afternoon. A Gangnam clinic blog (Roi Surgery, July 2026) states that once direction is set remotely, "your in-person consultation and surgery can often happen on the same day," with recommended stays of about 8 days for eyes and rhinoplasty (sutures day 7) and about 13 days for facelift, deposit via PayPal and balance on surgery day. id Hospital says same-day *reservations* are not available and consultations run from 13:00 because of surgery schedules. AB recommends 1-2 weeks in Korea and booking 1-2 months ahead; Banobagi recommends 5 days to 2 weeks, one month for two-jaw surgery.
- **Why it is flagged**: The Korean plastic surgery deaths covered in press involve post-operative monitoring failures (2018 anesthesia monitoring death; 2023-24 liposuction death with infection and inadequate post-op measures, per prosecutors). The ghost-surgery literature (Annals of Surgical Treatment and Research, 2018) notes foreign patients are affected and that substitute surgeons rarely appear in records. Korea Times (2026-02-25) quotes experts calling follow-up care after patients return home a gap and proposing telemedicine. Euronews (2023, on Botox clinics) quotes a Dubai clinic director advising patients to stay "two days to one week" to watch for complications. The FTC's 2026 actions show review manipulation and unfair prepaid refund terms were widespread, so same-trip decisions based on online reviews carry extra risk.
- **Practical guidance supported by sources**: get a written itemized quote (AB's own cost page lists what a good quote should state: surgery item, anesthesia plan, implant/graft, OR fee, pre-op checks, medication, post-op visits, interpreter, aftercare); confirm the consulting surgeon operates (Grand advertises a surgical name-verification system; Korea has required OR CCTV for general-anesthesia surgery per secondary sources, not verified here); plan the stay around suture removal (day 3-7 eyes, day 7 nose, 2 weeks lifting/liposuction per View) and avoid flying before that; expect final judgment at 6-12 months and no revision before ~6 months (Wonjin) or ~1 year (rhinoplasty per ASPS).
