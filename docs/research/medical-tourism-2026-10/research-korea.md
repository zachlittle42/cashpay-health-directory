# South Korea Medical Tourism for American Cash-Pay Patients: Research Report

Prepared 2026-10-30 for the VitalityScout destination rebuild. Every figure carries an inline citation [n] to the numbered source list at the end. Source types are labeled: OFFICIAL (Korean government, KHIDI, MOHW, MOJ, KTO, US CDC, FTC Korea), PRESS (established news outlets), PROFESSIONAL (medical societies), MARKETPLACE/AGENCY (booking platforms, agencies, clinic blogs; treat as marketing), FORUM (patient forums). Prices quoted from MARKETPLACE sources are indicative only and are flagged as such.

## 1. Market overview

**Volumes (OFFICIAL, Ministry of Health and Welfare / KHIDI).** Korea counted 2,011,822 foreign patients in 2025, up 71.9% from 1,170,467 in 2024, the first year above 2 million since statistics began in 2009. Cumulative since 2009: 7.06 million. Patient-visits (연환자) were 2.72 million [1][2][3]. Prior years: 605,768 (2023), 248,000 (2022), 146,000 (2021), ~110,000 (2020 pandemic low), 497,000 (2019) [4][5][6]. The government's own 2023 plan had targeted only 700,000 by 2027 [5].

US patients grew 70.4% in 2025, the fastest US growth since 2009 [2][7]. In 2024 the US count was about 102,000 (+32.2%), 8.7% of the total [8][9]. Americans are the fourth-largest source and the largest non-Asian source.

**Specialties, 2025 (OFFICIAL via PRESS reproduction of MOHW release) [3][10]:** dermatology 1.31 million (62.9%, +86.2%); plastic surgery 233,000 (11.2%, +64.3%); integrated internal medicine 192,000 (9.2%, +54.9%); health screening centers 65,000 (3.1%); dental +79.0%. Dermatology plus plastic surgery together were 74.1% of department-level counts in 2025, versus 68% in 2024 and 52% in 2023 [10]. Among US patients specifically, dermatology and plastic surgery were a lower 53.6% of department-level visits, meaning Americans are more spread across checkups, internal medicine, dental and other care than Asian visitors [10]. Note: department counts tally a patient once per department, so one person can appear more than once [10].

**Where care happens (OFFICIAL) [3]:** 87.7% of foreign patients were seen at clinic-level institutions (의원급), 3.6% at general hospitals, 3.0% at tertiary hospitals. Seoul accounted for 87.2% (about 1.76 million) and holds 62.5% (2,555) of institutions registered to treat foreign patients [2][3][11]. Busan 3.8%, Gyeonggi 2.7%, Jeju 2.3%, Incheon 1.3% [3].

**Gangnam concentration.** In 2024 Gangnam-gu drew nearly 380,000 foreign patients (+103% YoY) of Seoul's ~999,600; Gangnam, Seocho, Mapo, Jung and Songpa together took ~92% of Seoul's foreign patient visits (Seoul Metropolitan Government data, PRESS) [12]. In 2023 Gangnam-gu alone had 185,559 foreign patients, 30.6% of the national total, of whom 12% were American; dermatology was 48.2% and cosmetic surgery 22.7% of Gangnam visits [13]. 2007 clinic counts (dated): 728 plastic surgery clinics nationwide, 255 in Gangnam-gu, 163 in Sinsa-dong [14].

**Spending (OFFICIAL/KIET estimate).** Foreign patients and companions spent an estimated 12.5 trillion won (~$8.4B) in 2025, of which 3.3 trillion won was medical spending; estimated production effect 22.8 trillion won [2][3]. Foreign patients' medical spending was 1.24 trillion won in 2024, with plastic surgery and dermatology making up 77.3% of that [15].

**Stem cell and "anti-aging" (regulatory status, PRESS).** Korea's Act on the Safety and Support of Advanced Regenerative Medicine permits stem cell treatment only "within the system through clinical research and procedures" with review and long-term follow-up; a February 2025 amendment was expected to widen access but medical institutions report difficulty getting treatments approved [41][42]. The Ministry estimates 10,000–20,000 Koreans travel abroad (mostly Japan) each year for stem cell infusions and has said such overseas programs may violate the Medical Service Act; a Korean woman in her 60s died in March 2026 after an autologous fat-derived stem cell IV infusion at a Tokyo clinic supplied by a Korean cell company [41][43]. Practical implication for Americans: a Korean clinic marketing unrestricted stem cell "anti-aging" injections is either operating inside a registered clinical-research protocol or outside the law. Treat marketing claims (e.g., Bookimed's "stem cell therapy is legal in Korea") as unreliable [44].

## 3. How the system works

**Legal framework (OFFICIAL).** The Act on Support for Overseas Expansion of Healthcare System and Attraction of International Patients requires any medical institution that recruits foreign patients, and any agency ("facilitator") that refers them, to register with the government and to display its registration certificate on premises [45][46]. Since 2016 registered institutions must carry medical malpractice liability insurance (over 100M won/year for clinics and small hospitals, over 200M won for general hospitals); Medical Korea reports 100% of registered institutions insured [46][47]. Unregistered brokers face up to 3 years in prison or a 30M won fine; informants can earn up to 10M won [46]. As of August 2023 there were 3,800+ registered institutions and 2,300+ registered facilitators [47]; in 2025 Seoul alone held 2,555 registered institutions (62.5% of the national total, implying roughly 4,100 nationwide) [11].

**Medical Korea portal (OFFICIAL, KHIDI + KTO).** medicalkorea.or.kr publishes searchable lists at /en/registeredhospitals and /en/registeredfacilitators, a KAHF accreditation list at /en/kahfAccreditedHospitals, visa guidance, and a downloadable "Medical Dispute Resolution Guidelines for Foreign Patients" brochure. Contacts: KHIDI +82-43-713-8000, Seoul Medical Korea desk +82-2-1577-7129, Incheon Airport desk +82-32-743-3506/3507 [47][48]. Consumer check: ask the clinic and the agency for their registration certificate numbers and match them on the portal.

**KAHF accreditation (OFFICIAL).** The Korea Accreditation Program for Hospitals Serving Foreign Patients has run since 2017 under MOHW/KHIDI with roughly 130–150 standards; a provider can be registered without being KAHF-accredited [49][50]. The portal's KAHF/accredited list showed 26 entries on 2026-10-30, including CHA Fertility Center Seoul Station, JK Plastic Surgery Center (Gangnam), AB Plastic Surgery (Seocho), HanGil Eye Hospital (Incheon), Sejong Hospital (Bucheon/Incheon), Busan St. Mary's and several regional university hospitals [51]. Thirteen hospitals held KAHF as of a 2023 Busan report [49]. KOIHA is Korea's separate national hospital accreditation body [49].

**JCI accreditation.** No authoritative current count could be retrieved (JCI's directory blocked automated access). Press and JCI's own materials have named Severance Hospital (first in Korea, 2007), Gangnam Severance, Korea University Anam, Seoul St. Mary's, Gachon Gil, Inha University Hospital, Chonnam National University Hwasun Hospital and Seoul Wooridul Spine Hospital as JCI-accredited at various dates [52][53][54]. Samsung Medical Center's JCI status appears only in a third-party directory and is **unverified** [55]. Treat any JCI claim as "verify on the JCI directory."

**Interpreters.** Korea has run a national medical interpreter training program since 2009 and a Medical Interpreter Proficiency Test since 2016 (first cohort: 50 of 138 passed; certificate issued by the Minister of Health and Welfare) [56][57]. The portal lists each registered provider's supported languages [48]. Interpreters are expensive for clinics with few foreign patients, so many rely on agency coordinators [14].

**VAT refund: ended.** Foreign patients could reclaim 10% VAT on cosmetic and dermatology procedures from April 2016 through December 31, 2025. The Ministry of Economy and Finance dropped the provision from the 2025 tax plan in July 2025 and the National Assembly omitted it from the December 2025 revision [15][58]. Refunds hit 196.4 billion won in 2025 (1.72 million claims) and 400.8 billion won cumulative [10]. **As of 2026 there is no VAT refund on cosmetic procedures in Korea**; any site or clinic still advertising one is out of date [58][59]. Retail shopping VAT refunds continue [59].

**Visas for Americans (OFFICIAL, MOFA/MOJ).** US passport holders may stay up to 90 days visa-free for tourism and similar short visits [60]. K-ETA is waived for US citizens through December 31, 2026 (MOJ extension); no decision for 2027 had been announced as of October 2026; the separate e-Arrival Card is still required for those without a K-ETA [61][62]. The C-3-3 (C-3-M) medical visa covers treatment-and-travel stays of 90 days or less and the G-1-M covers longer treatment; a visa-exempt American typically does not need a C-3-3 for a cosmetic trip under 90 days [63]. From October 1, 2026 the Ministry of Justice designates 112 "outstanding" institutions and facilitators (89 medical facilities, 23 agencies; 90 in greater Seoul) whose patients get 3-day electronic visa processing and financial-document waivers; designations run through September 30, 2027 [64][65]. C-3-3 visas issued via certified channels rose 40% to 9,804 (Oct 2025–Aug 2026) [65].

**Telemedicine (OFFICIAL, new).** A May 26, 2026 amendment legalizes cross-border telemedicine for foreign patients (first-time remote consultations, pre-travel counseling, post-return follow-up, remote prescriptions) at designated institutions, effective after a 12-month grace period, i.e., mid-2027 [66].

## 4. Safety and consumer protection

**Ghost (shadow) surgery and the CCTV law.** Kwon Dae-hee, 24, died in October 2016 after a jaw-contouring surgery in Gangnam in which CCTV showed a general practitioner without plastic surgery training took over the operation; the surgeon was sentenced to three years in 2021 [67][68]. His mother's campaign produced the "Kwon Dae-hee law": a 2021 Medical Service Act amendment, in force September 25, 2023, requiring every operating room where patients are under general anesthesia or otherwise unconscious to have closed-circuit cameras (no external network). Recording is mandatory when the patient or guardian requests it (emergencies excepted); audio requires mutual consent; footage must be kept at least 30 days; viewing requires a court or investigative request or consent of both patient and staff; leaking or tampering carries up to 5 years or 50M won [69][70][71]. The Korea Medical Association filed a constitutional challenge in September 2023 [70]. Practical point for patients: **you must request recording in writing before surgery**; the law does not record by default.

**Scale of harm (PRESS citing OFFICIAL data).** Korea Consumer Agency counted 226 people injured, needing re-operation, or dying after plastic surgery in 2016–2020 [72]. The National Forensic Service analyzed 50 cosmetic-procedure deaths in 2016–2024; 23 (46%) were anesthesia-related and 22 of those occurred at clinics [73]. Any licensed Korean physician may legally perform cosmetic surgery; only board-certified plastic surgeons may call their practice a "plastic surgery clinic" [19].

**Anesthesia staffing gap (PRESS citing HIRA data, Oct 2026).** Of 2,777 registered plastic surgery and dermatology clinics, only 84 (3%) had an anesthesiologist on staff as of July 2026 (plastic surgery 5.6%, dermatology 1%); current law does not require an anesthesiologist for sedation ("sleep anesthesia"), and none of 20 southern-Seoul clinics reviewed that advertised sedation had one [73]. A man in his 30s died in 2025 after a propofol-sedated cosmetic procedure in Suwon; the doctor was referred for prosecution in February 2026 [74].

**Foreign-patient deaths and prosecutions.** A Chinese woman in her 20s died in January 2024 after three full-body liposuction sessions in two weeks at a Gangnam clinic; the surgeon was indicted in June 2026 and is separately on trial for a 2018 death of another Chinese liposuction patient under anesthesia [75][76]. Hong Kong heiress Bonnie Evita Law died during liposuction in Seoul in 2020, reportedly introduced via an illegal broker [19][77]. China's embassy in Seoul issued a public warning about cosmetic surgery risks in Korea in January 2024 [78].

**Dispute resolution (OFFICIAL + PRESS).** The Korea Medical Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Agency (K-MEDI) offers counseling, mediation, arbitration and medical accident appraisal; it publishes English application forms for patients, an English notice on an arbitration clause hospitals can insert in foreign-patient contracts (award has the force of a court judgment), and a weekday phone line 1670-2545 / +82-2-6210-0080, kmedi@k-medi.or.kr [79]. Foreign patients may use it, but consultations are small: 113 (2020), 133 (2024), 152 through August 2025, a record; 2025 cases led by cosmetic surgery (13) and dentistry (13) [80]. KHIDI's Medical Korea Support Center logged 51,012 inquiries in 2024 and 214 dissatisfaction/dispute cases (165 in H1 2025), and illegal-broker reports rose from 13 (2020) to 67 (2024); the center has six contract staff in two branches [80]. Korea's FTC in July 2026 ordered 15 clinics to delete unfair prepaid-treatment refund clauses and said consumers may be charged at most a 10% penalty on early termination under the Consumer Dispute Settlement Standards [81].

**US government guidance.** CDC advises seeing a travel-medicine clinician 4–6 weeks before, checking credentials, arranging evacuation insurance, carrying records, and delaying air travel 10–14 days after major surgery (especially chest) because of clot risk; it warns of drug-resistant organisms and continuity-of-care gaps [82]. The State Department's South Korea page (Level 1) could not be fetched; OSAC's mirror mentions a physician shortage but no cosmetic-surgery-specific warning [83]. ASPS warns that complications after surgery abroad often fall to US surgeons unfamiliar with the technique used, that revision costs can exceed the original US price, and that patients generally have little legal recourse abroad [84][85].

**Antimicrobial resistance and blood (OFFICIAL via PRESS).** KDCA recorded 49,053 carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales (CRE) cases in 2025 (+15.8%) and 944 CRE deaths, the most of any notifiable disease [86]. Korea's blood supply is screened with NAT for HIV/HCV since 2005 and HBV since 2012; reserves periodically dip below the five-day alert threshold (e.g., 4.4 days during a flu outbreak) [87][88]. Neither is a reason to avoid Korea, but both support CDC's point about hospital-acquired infection risk and asking about transfusion plans for major surgery.

## 5. Practical logistics for Americans

**Flights.** Nonstop LAX–ICN runs about 13–14 hours westbound, 11–12 back; JFK–ICN about 14.5 hours; United's new daily EWR–ICN (launched September 4, 2026) is scheduled just under 16 hours out and 13h45 back [89][90]. Korean Air's LAX–Seoul page listed round-trip economy from $1,094 (snapshot, not an average) [91]. Nonstop gateways reported for 2026 include LAX, SFO, SEA, JFK, EWR, ATL, DFW, ORD, LAS, SLC, HNL; aggregator data conflict on ATL/ORD/DFW carriers, so verify with airlines [90][92].

**Where to stay.** Most cosmetic clinics cluster in Gangnam-gu (Sinsa, Apgujeong, Cheongdam, Nonhyeon) and Seocho-gu. Booking-site snapshots put mid-range Gangnam hotels at roughly $100–200/night, budget near $60, luxury $250+ [93]. Seoul launched a "Seoul Medical-Friendly Accommodation" certification pilot in September 2026 for hotels serving foreign patients (criteria: hospital access, 24-hour emergency response, wheelchair routes), to be listed on the city's five-language medical tourism site [94].

**Length of stay and flying home.** Agency guidance converges on 7–14 days for rhinoplasty and most facial surgery, with splint/stitch removal around day 7–10 and a surgeon clearance before flying; facial contouring and multi-procedure trips often run 2–3 weeks; CDC's general rule is 10–14 days after major surgery [82][95]. IVF cycles need about 2–3 weeks on site [39]. Health screenings are 3–4 hours (centers) to overnight (hospital VIP packages) with results in days [27][29].

**Payment, deposits, refunds.** Clinics commonly require a deposit of about 10% (sometimes a flat ~$1,000) to hold a date; consultation fees range from free to ~10,000 won; foreign cards are accepted but cash discounts are reported [96] (FORUM/AGENCY). Deposit-refund disputes are a leading complaint; the FTC's July 2026 action says only a 10% penalty may be deducted on early termination of prepaid treatment, and the FTC is drafting a standard cosmetic-medicine contract [81]. Get the written cancellation schedule before paying.

**Follow-up in the US.** ASPS: US surgeons may not know what implants or techniques were used; bring operative notes, implant cards and photos; revisions are out of pocket [84][85]. Korea's 2026 telemedicine law will allow legal remote follow-up with the Korean surgeon from mid-2027 [66].

**Seasonality.** No official monthly foreign-patient data were found. Korean domestic demand peaks in winter school breaks [97]; **unsourced** for foreign seasonality.

**Exchange rate.** The won traded around 1,440–1,470 per dollar in early 2026, weakened to ~1,556 on June 5, 2026, then strengthened to ~1,332 by October 16, 2026, so dollar-denominated prices have risen ~10% since mid-year [98][99].

## 6. Red flags and how to vet

1. **Unregistered brokers.** Soliciting or referring foreign patients without facilitator registration is illegal; Al Jazeera was approached within minutes by uncertified facilitators in online groups, and broker fees are described as "exorbitant" [19][80]. Verify the agency on medicalkorea.or.kr/en/registeredfacilitators [48].
2. **Any-doctor cosmetic surgery.** Ask for the operating surgeon's board certification (성형외과 전문의) and confirm in writing that the named surgeon will perform the entire operation [19].
3. **Sedation without an anesthesiologist.** 97% of cosmetic clinics have none on staff; ask who administers and monitors anesthesia and whether an anesthesiologist is present for general anesthesia [73].
4. **No CCTV recording request.** Recording is only on request; request it in writing [69][70].
5. **Manipulated before/after photos.** Korea's FTC fined two Gangnam clinics (Secret, Faceline) for exaggerated photos [100]; government has proposed banning before/after and celebrity-image ads [101].
6. **Incentivized reviews.** In July 2026 the FTC issued corrective orders to View, DA and AB Plastic Surgery for discounted surgery in exchange for undisclosed positive reviews (2018–May 2026) [102]; influencer contracts requiring positive language before results are known have been documented [19].
7. **Refund-for-silence.** One American patient received a refund only after deleting her review and signing an NDA; defamation suits for true negative reviews are a documented risk in Korea [19].
8. **Conveyor-belt consults.** Being rushed into surgery within an hour of consultation with no in-house interpreter was reported [19].
9. **Dual pricing.** A Korean investigation found four of ten Gangnam clinics quoting foreigners nearly double local prices; many clinics now publish "same price" pledges [103]. Ask for an itemized won quote.
10. **Stem cell / "anti-aging" injections** outside a registered research protocol [41][43].

**Confirm in writing:** surgeon name and specialty; anesthesia type, provider and monitoring; itemized price in won including anesthesia, facility, implants, medications, follow-up; cancellation schedule; CCTV recording request; malpractice insurer; registration numbers; interpreter availability; what happens if revision is needed.

## 8. Frequently asked questions (sourced answers)

1. **Is Korea really cheaper than the US?** Yes for most facial cosmetic surgery, dental implants, screenings and SMILE (often 40–70% below US surgeon-fee ranges), less so for breast augmentation and facelifts once travel is added [16][17][18][23][32]. One Korean-American reported $9,000 vs $50,000 quoted at home; another said flights and lodging erased the savings [96].
2. **Do I need a visa?** No for stays under 90 days; K-ETA waived through Dec 31, 2026; file the e-Arrival Card [60][61][62].
3. **Can I get the 10% VAT refund?** No. It ended Dec 31, 2025 [58][59].
4. **How do I verify a clinic or agency?** Check medicalkorea.or.kr registered lists and ask to see the registration certificate [47][48].
5. **Will the surgeon I consult do my surgery?** Not guaranteed; ghost surgery is why the CCTV law exists. Get the surgeon's name in the consent form and request recording [67][69][70].
6. **Do I need an anesthesiologist?** Law doesn't require one for sedation; 97% of cosmetic clinics have none. Ask [73].
7. **How long should I stay?** 7–14 days for rhinoplasty; 2–3 weeks for contouring or multiple procedures; CDC says 10–14 days before flying after major surgery [82][95].
8. **Will my US insurance cover it?** Elective cosmetic care is not covered; CDC advises evacuation coverage; complications back home are often paid by the patient or Medicaid [82][85].
9. **What if something goes wrong?** Registered clinics carry malpractice insurance; K-MEDI offers English mediation forms; lawsuits are slow and defamation risk exists for public complaints [19][46][79].
10. **Do staff speak English?** Registered-provider listings show interpreter languages; certified medical interpreters exist but most clinics rely on coordinators [48][56].
11. **How big a deposit?** Typically ~10%; FTC says maximum 10% penalty on early termination [81][96].
12. **Are before/after photos and reviews trustworthy?** FTC has sanctioned clinics for both manipulated photos and undisclosed paid reviews [100][102].
13. **Is Gangnam the only place?** 87% of foreign patients are seen in Seoul; Gangnam-gu alone took ~380,000 in 2024 [2][12].
14. **How many Americans go?** 173,363 in 2025, up 70.4% [1][7].
15. **What do Americans go for?** Dermatology and plastic surgery (53.6% of US department visits) plus checkups, internal medicine and dental [10].
16. **Are health checkups worth the trip?** Screening-center packages run ~$350–1,520 and take 3–4 hours; US patients report sub-$600 evaluations that caught missed findings [27][31]; hospital VIP overnight packages cost more [29][30].
17. **Is stem cell therapy legal in Korea?** Only inside the regenerative-medicine law's clinical-research framework [41][42].
18. **Can single women do IVF in Korea?** Agency sources say no (married couples only, surrogacy illegal); verify with clinic [39].
19. **Can I fly right after a facelift or contouring?** Clinics clear at stitch removal (day 7–10); longer for general anesthesia and body work [82][95].
20. **How do I pay?** Foreign credit cards accepted; cash discounts reported; get an itemized won quote [96][103].
21. **Is Korean tap water/food/blood safe?** Blood is NAT-screened; CRE infections are rising nationally, so ask about infection control for inpatient stays [86][87].
22. **Will my Korean surgeon follow up after I'm home?** Legal cross-border telemedicine begins after a 12-month grace period from May 2026 [66].
23. **Are prices going up?** VAT refund loss adds 10%; won strengthening from ~1,470 to ~1,330/USD in 2026 adds roughly 10% more in dollar terms [58][98][99].

## 9. Trends 2025–2026

- **Explosive growth, cosmetic-skewed.** 2M+ patients; 74% dermatology/plastics; clinic-level care 87.7% [3][10].
- **VAT refund ended Dec 31, 2025**; plastic surgeons' association warned of patient loss to Thailand, Singapore, Hainan; analysts expected limited short-term impact [15][58].
- **Won strengthened** from ~1,556 (June 5, 2026) to ~1,332 (Oct 16, 2026) per dollar, raising dollar prices [98][99].
- **Telemedicine for foreign patients legalized** (May 26, 2026; effective after 12 months) [66].
- **Visa network expanded** to 112 MOJ-designated institutions/agencies from Oct 1, 2026, with 3-day e-visas and regional push [64][65].
