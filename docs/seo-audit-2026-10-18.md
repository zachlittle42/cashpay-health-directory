# SEO audit — October 18, 2026

**Bottom line: nothing technical is blocking Google. The blocker is what Google finds once it arrives.** All 20 live-sampled URLs are clean: 200 status, self-canonical, no noindex, content in the server HTML. The live sitemap is byte-identical to the build. Google's "Discovered – currently not indexed" state is therefore a crawl-priority and site-quality judgment. Roughly 229 of 797 indexable URLs dilute the site, and several YMYL trust signals overstate what the site actually does. Fix the inventory and the trust claims before publishing more pages.

## Findings

### 1. Google's inclusion gap is a quality signal, not a technical block

- **Clean technical baseline** (indexation-1). Every page in the 12-URL Google inspection sample, plus 5 provider pages and 3 city pages, returned 200 with a self-canonical in both the HTML and the `Link` header. None had a meta robots or `X-Robots-Tag` block, and every one had its H1 and prices in the server HTML. Pages Google indexed and pages it did not are technically indistinguishable.
- **Only about a third of the indexable inventory clearly clears a low-authority YMYL bar** (quality-1). 266 of 797 indexable URLs clear it, 300 are borderline, and 229 dilute it, including 106 that are in the sitemap. The guides are the asset: they are 27% of indexable URLs but hold 75% of the unique text, with essentially no duplication (mean pairwise Jaccard 0.012). The ED guide itself is substantive (1,262 unique words).
- **Where the dilution comes from:**
  - **315 `/providers/*` pages** (quality-4, indexation-2). They are indexable and linked but left out of the sitemap, which is a mixed signal. They make up 39.5% of indexable URLs but only 9.7% of unique text, with a median of 118 unique words and zero in-content outlinks. 74 are still stamped "Last verified: December 2024", and 36 are off-mission (insurers, pharma companies, FDA databases).
  - **54 city-grid pages** with 3–4 clinics (quality-2). They pass `MIN_CLINICS_FOR_INDEX = 3` in `src/lib/indexability.ts` but carry a median of 33–96 strictly unique words.
  - **30 single-city state pages** (quality-5). Each near-duplicates its only city: state-to-city Jaccard is 0.42–0.61, and 73–84% of the state page's shingles appear on the city page.
  - **California med-spa pages** (quality-3). These are chain stubs built from one description template (`src/data/medspa-chains.ts`). ✅ Raw 5-shingle Jaccard for Los Angeles vs San Diego is **0.73** (0.97 with city names masked). They came from the June seed and now read as doorway pages. Texas is less duplicated (Houston vs Dallas 0.21) but reportedly reuses four rotating independent-clinic blurbs (unverified).
- **The prior noindex of `/traditional-healthcare/*` and `/health-systems/*` was correct** (quality-6). However, sitewide navigation still sends its strongest links to noindexed hubs (indexation-3):
  - `Navigation.tsx:13` links "By State" to `/traditional-healthcare`.
  - `SidebarNav.tsx:19-20` links to `/lose-weight` and `/balance-hormones`, both noindex.
  - Overall, 9% of internal link edges from indexable pages point at noindexed URLs.

### 2. Trust claims on YMYL pages overstate reality

- ✅ **144 MedicalWebPage blocks claim `reviewedBy: "VitalityScout Editorial Team"`** (schema-1). That reviewer appears nowhere in the visible text. `lastReviewed` always equals the authoring date. `/editorial-policy` itself says research "does not mean the page has been reviewed by a clinician." This is the most directly self-contradicting signal on the site.
- **Hardcoded template `dateModified` values contradict the visible "verified" dates** (schema-3, 187 pages). For example, `weight-loss/[state]/[city]/page.tsx:95` hardcodes `2026-02-01` while the page shows "verified … Jul 2026". 55 sitemap URLs carry pre-2026 dates.
- **Stale years** (onpage-3). 34 titles still say 2024 or 2025, including the Google-indexed `/guides/at-home-lab-testing-guide`. 126 pages show "verified/updated Dec 2024" stamps. 153 titles hard-code "2026" and will all go stale on Jan 1, 2027.
- **Inconsistent competitor prices** (onpage-9). For example, Superpower is listed at $199/yr on `/guides/function-health-review` and `/labs`, but at $349 on two sibling guides.
- **`MedicalBusiness` on all 315 provider pages** (schema-2), including Pfizer, UnitedHealthcare and Drugs@FDA. 129 of them have no address.
- ✅ **The Article publisher logo returns 404** (schema-4). 22 references point to `https://vitalityscout.com/logo.png`, and `public/` contains no logo. There is also no sitewide Organization node.

### 3. Snippets and sharing

- ✅ **The #1 traffic page's snippet changed right after its breakout** (onpage-1). Commit `b1e6e6b` (Sept 19) replaced `Online ED Treatment (2026): $20-$90/mo, Hims vs Ro vs BlueChew` with `…Providers, Prices & Prescription Options` (68 characters, likely truncated). The old title was live during the window when Bing CTR rose from 0.69% to 8.26%. The change hasn't been measured since. This is the first thing to check when fresh Bing data arrives.
- ✅ **803 of 807 pages declare the homepage as their `og:url`** and inherit the homepage og:title and og:description. No page has an `og:image` (onpage-5). Root cause: the root `openGraph`/`twitter` objects in `src/app/layout.tsx`. Every social share and some AI/answer-engine previews show the homepage.
- **High-impression, low-CTR pages** (onpage-2):
  - Function Health review: 8,756 impressions, 0.14% CTR. About 88% of those impressions are the bare brand query, so expectations should be modest.
  - Ro weight-loss cost: 0.61%.
  - MRI without insurance: 0.60%, down from 1.61% at a stable position.
  - For comparison, LASIK converts 23× better than Function at the same position 7.
- **Length**: 294 titles exceed 65 characters, mostly because of the ` | VitalityScout` suffix and programmatic templates (onpage-4). 282 descriptions exceed 165 characters, and the provider template pastes the full profile text into the description (onpage-8).

### 4. Crawl hygiene (small, cheap)

- **`www.vitalityscout.com` is not attached to the Vercel project** (indexation-4), so the middleware's www→apex 308 can never run. `cashpay-health-directory.vercel.app` → apex works.
- **The sitemap has no `lastmod`** even though 441 of its 480 URLs carry a `dateModified`; `priority` and `changefreq` are uniform (indexation-5). Fix the dates (schema-3) first, then emit them.
- **IndexNow is ad hoc** (indexation-6). There are two key files, one recorded manual ping on Sept 19, and nothing runs on deploy, even though Bing drives almost all traffic.
- **Stale links**: 48 internal links still point at the redirected `/dexa` (indexation-9). Four internal links hit 404s, including `/oncology` and `/hair-transplant` (indexation-8). `/search` is indexable with no canonical (indexation-7).

## Content expansion: what to build next

The coverage lens didn't run, but the quality lens mapped the obvious gaps (quality-11). These primary US cash-pay services have **no page at all**:

- Hearing aids, vasectomy, physical therapy, chiropractic, Invisalign/orthodontics, teeth whitening
- Mammogram, X-ray, STD testing
- Birth control, hair-loss Rx (finasteride/minoxidil), ADHD telehealth
- Direct primary care/concierge
- Hyperbaric oxygen, cryotherapy, acupuncture/massage, PRP
- Glasses/contacts

The format that has worked on this site is the **national "X cost without insurance" guide**, not new city grids. Every traction URL is a guide or hub. Suggested sequence, each item adjacent to an existing traction cluster:

1. **Hair-loss Rx and birth-control telehealth cost.** These share the Hims/Ro provider set with the ED page.
2. **STD testing cost; mammogram and X-ray cost without insurance.** These extend the labs and imaging clusters Google already indexes.
3. **Direct primary care membership cost.** This enriches `/primary_care`.
4. **Clear aligners vs Invisalign cost.** This extends dental.
5. **OTC hearing aids cost.**

**Publishing gate:**
- One guide per distinct intent; check existing guides first.
- At least 1,200 unique words and dated, verified prices from 5 or more providers.
- In-content links from the relevant indexed hub.
- Ship new guides only in sprints that also cut the indexable count, so total indexable inventory goes down while quality goes up.

## What's working — don't break it

- **Technical base**: clean live status, canonicals (HTML and header), robots, and redirects; the `.vercel.app` → apex 308; a real 404 status; a sitemap that exactly matches the build.
- **Content and structure**: the guides (deep, unique, near-zero duplication); one H1 per page; unique meta descriptions everywhere; zero JSON-LD parse errors; no self-serving `aggregateRating`.
- **Earlier decisions**: the noindex of `/traditional-healthcare/*` and `/health-systems/*`; the honest `/editorial-policy`; the dated, sourced pattern in `src/lib/ed-offers.ts` and the price index, which other templates should copy.
- **ED guide**: keep its URL and core intent unchanged.
